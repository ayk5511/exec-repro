\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The corpus}\label{sec:corpus}

The corpus is 1,754 machine-learning papers in quantitative finance, harvested
from arXiv along two axes. The six quantitative-finance categories (q-fin.CP, ST,
TR, PM, RM, MF) were read to exhaustion under a machine-learning filter, and the
general machine-learning categories (cs.LG, stat.ML, econ.EM, cs.CE) were queried
by finance topic, which is where finance work that is not indexed under q-fin
lives. Of these papers, 459 advertise a code URL on a recognised hosting service
and constitute the execution sample.

Code URLs were recovered in two passes, and the difference between them is
itself a result. Reading abstracts alone, the standard method, found 98. Reading
the rendered full text of each paper found a further 361, so nearly four-fifths
of the advertised repositories in this literature are invisible to
abstract-level extraction. Any disclosure rate computed from abstracts is a
severe underestimate.

Extracted URLs required normalisation before use. URLs harvested from prose
routinely carry a trailing sentence period or a wrapping bracket, and treating
those characters as part of the address would have reported live repositories as
dead.

Two features of the corpus bound the claims. It is an arXiv sample, so venues
whose authors do not preprint are absent, and it is described throughout as
arXiv ML-finance rather than as the ML-finance literature. And although the
harvest reaches back to 2019, it remains weighted toward recent work.

Three features of the curve carry the paper's content.

\emph{Link rot is real but modest.} Twenty-four of 459 advertised repositories
(5.2\%) no longer resolve. This is the failure mode most often assumed to
dominate, and it is the smallest of the large drops. Because a transient
resolver failure is indistinguishable from a dead domain on a single attempt,
transport-level failures are retried before being recorded; without that, one
repository in this corpus would have been reported as rotted when it was live.

\emph{The first large drop is environment declaration.} Of the 427 repositories
that clone, 176 ship no dependency specification of any kind, taking the sample
from 93.0\% to 54.7\% in a single rung. Nothing exotic is required to clear it: a
\texttt{requirements.txt} suffices.

\emph{The second large drop is environment construction.} Of the 251
repositories that declare dependencies, half again fail to install them, taking
the sample to 27.7\%. This is substantially decay rather than negligence, and
\Cref{tab:failure_mix} shows the mechanism.

The environment failures divide into three roughly comparable mechanisms.
\texttt{package\_not\_found} (45) is a pinned dependency that has left the index;
\texttt{dependency\_resolution\_conflict} (38) is a pin set that no longer admits
a solution; \texttt{build\_from\_source\_failed} (34) is a package with no
current wheel that fails to compile. All three are properties of elapsed time
rather than of the authors' diligence at submission, which means executability
decays after publication. It also means this measurement is timestamped: the same
corpus scored two years hence will score lower.

At the execution rung, the sixteen \texttt{missing\_data\_file} outcomes are
substantive. Network access is denied during execution by design, so a repository
that fetches its inputs at run time is recorded here rather than being rescued by
our connectivity: the artefact as released does not carry what it needs.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the entry-point heuristic}\label{sec:sensitivity}

Identifying what to run is the most discretionary step, so the study is run in two
configurations. Restricting to conventionally named scripts finds an entry point
in 39 repositories; additionally accepting a command documented in the
repository's own README finds 64. The looser specification raises the number that
execute from 4 to 9 and the number that emit output from 2 to 7.

The two configurations agree exactly on rungs 2 through 6, which are decided
before any entry-point logic runs. That agreement is checked by the audit script
and is the study's internal-consistency control: a discrepancy there would mean
the campaign was not deterministic and no figure above could be quoted.

The gap between the columns is the honest measure of how much of the upper curve
is our conservatism rather than the literature's. It is large in relative terms
and small in absolute terms: on either specification, fewer than one paper in
fifty executes to completion unassisted.

\subsection{Replication on an independent sample}\label{sec:replication}

An earlier campaign scored a 154-paper sample drawn only from the q-fin
categories. The present corpus is three times larger and reaches papers through
the general machine-learning categories, which the earlier sample could not see.

The comparison is restricted to rungs 2 through 6, which are decided before any
entry-point logic applies and are therefore unaffected by the heuristic revisions
made between the two campaigns. On those rungs the samples agree closely:
resolution 92.2\% against 94.8\%, cloning 87.7\% against 93.0\%, dependency
declaration 53.2\% against 54.7\%, and environment construction 26.6\% against
27.7\%. Both of the paper's principal findings, the size of the declaration drop
and the size of the construction drop, sit inside this range.

We do not claim replication above rung 6. The two campaigns used different
entry-point rules, the later one stricter, so the upper figures are not
like-for-like and the difference between them measures our revision rather than
the literature.

Because the second sample is larger and differently sourced, the agreement on the
mechanically determined rungs is evidence that the shape of the curve is a
property of the literature rather than of the particular papers first drawn.

\subsection{Rung 10}\label{sec:rung10_results}

Seven repositories produced output and are therefore eligible for the final rung,
which asks whether an emitted number matches a headline number in the paper. Seven
is too few to support a rate, and we report no percentage for rung 10. The
worksheet pairing each artefact's captured stdout with its paper's numeric claims
is released with the corpus so the adjudication can be inspected and disputed.

That the sample thins to seven from 459 is itself the finding: the question the
rung was designed to answer is, for this corpus, almost entirely unanswerable,
because almost nothing gets far enough to ask it of.
